How the last working day is counted

If your notice period is 90 days and it starts on the day you resign, your last working day is the 90th day, counting the resignation day as day 1. For example, resigning on 1 November with 90 days' notice gives a last working day of 29 January. If notice starts the next day, it moves one day later.

For notice in months, the last day is the day before the same date that many months later: resigning on 15 November with 2 months' notice ends on 14 January.

Notice buyout

Many companies let you leave early by paying for the days you don't serve. The usual formula is:

Buyout = days not served × monthly salary ÷ 30

Some companies use basic pay, others gross pay, and some divide by the days in that month. Often the new employer reimburses the buyout; ask during negotiation.

Tips for a smooth exit

  • Resign in writing (email is fine) and keep a copy with the date.
  • Ask HR to confirm the last working day and any early release in writing.
  • Plan a handover document early. Managers are far more willing to release early when work is covered.
  • Check how unused leave is treated: encashed, adjusted against notice, or lapsed.

Frequently asked questions

Do weekends count in the notice period?

Yes, almost always. Notice periods are in calendar days, so weekends and holidays count.

Can my company refuse early release?

Yes; the notice period is part of your contract. Most companies agree to a buyout or a shorter notice if the handover is done.
