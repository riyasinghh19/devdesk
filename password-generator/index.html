    What makes a password strong

    Length beats complexity. Each extra character multiplies the number of guesses needed. A random 16-character password with letters, numbers and symbols would take longer than the age of the universe to brute-force. An 8-character one can fall in hours.

    Randomness matters more than cleverness. "Riya@1998" looks complex but follows a pattern attackers try first: name, symbol, birth year.

    Passwords or passphrases?

    A passphrase like Mango-Ticket-Silver-Plant-42 is easier to type on a phone and to remember, and four or five random words are as strong as a long random password. Use passphrases for things you type by hand, such as your laptop login or password manager. Use random passwords for everything stored in a password manager.

    Good habits

    • Use a different password for every site. A leak on one site then can't unlock the others.
    • Use a password manager (Bitwarden, 1Password, or the one in your browser or phone).
    • Turn on two-factor authentication for email, banking and work accounts.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is it safe to generate passwords on a website?

    This page creates passwords in your browser with crypto.getRandomValues, the same secure generator browsers use for encryption. Nothing is sent over the network or stored. You can even disconnect from the internet and it still works.

    Why avoid look-alike characters?

    If you'll ever read the password out or type it from paper, 0 and O or l and 1 cause mistakes. Leaving them out costs very little strength.
