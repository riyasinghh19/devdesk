          The formula

          Action verb + what you did + how + measurable result. Recruiters skim a resume in seconds; a bullet that starts with a strong verb and ends with a number gets read.

          Where to find your numbers

          • Time: hours saved, faster runtime, shorter turnaround ("from 3 days to 4 hours").
          • Money: cost cut, revenue influenced, budget managed.
          • Scale: rows, users, tables, reports, team size, number of clients.
          • Quality: fewer errors, higher accuracy, uptime, customer rating.

          Estimates are fine if they're honest and you can explain them in an interview. "About 40%" beats no number.

          Words to avoid

          "Responsible for", "worked on", "helped with", "involved in" and "duties included" describe a job, not an achievement. Replace them with what you actually did.

          Frequently asked questions

          How many bullets per job?

          3–5 for recent roles, 1–3 for older ones. Put the most impressive one first.

          Should bullets end with a full stop?

          Either is fine. Just be consistent across the resume.

          Will this help with ATS?

          Strong bullets help people; keywords help ATS. Use the job description's words for tools and skills, then check with the ATS score checker.
