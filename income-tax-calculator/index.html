Key rules the calculator applies

  • Standard deduction: ₹75,000 in the new regime, ₹50,000 in the old.
  • Section 87A rebate: no tax in the new regime if taxable income is up to ₹12 lakh, with marginal relief just above it. In the old regime, no tax up to ₹5 lakh.
  • So a salary of up to ₹12.75 lakh pays no tax in the new regime.
  • Surcharge: 10% above ₹50 lakh, 15% above ₹1 crore, 25% above ₹2 crore (the maximum in the new regime), 37% above ₹5 crore in the old regime, with marginal relief.
  • Health and education cess: 4% on tax plus surcharge.

Which regime should you choose?

The new regime wins for most salaried people now. The old regime only wins if your deductions are large: typically full 80C, health insurance, a big HRA exemption and home loan interest together. The calculator shows the deductions you'd need for the old regime to break even.

Frequently asked questions

Can I switch between regimes every year?

Salaried people can choose each year when filing the return. People with business income can switch back to the old regime only once.

Does this cover capital gains?

No. Capital gains on shares and mutual funds are taxed at special rates and the 87A rebate doesn't apply to them. This calculator is for salary and regular income.

Is this tax advice?

No. It's an estimate to help you compare. For filing, use the Income Tax Department's portal or speak to a chartered accountant.
