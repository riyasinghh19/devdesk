How SIP returns are calculated

Each monthly instalment grows for the months left until the end. With a monthly rate i (annual return ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n months, a fixed SIP of P grows to:

FV = P × ((1 + i)^n − 1) / i × (1 + i)

This assumes you invest at the start of each month, which is how most SIP calculators from fund houses work. With a step-up, the calculator simply raises the instalment every 12 months and adds up each one's growth.

Why step-up matters

₹10,000 a month for 20 years at 12% grows to about ₹1 crore. Raise it by 10% every year and it grows to about ₹2 crore. Most of the extra comes from the later, larger instalments, so it fits naturally with yearly appraisals.

What return should I assume?

  • Large-cap or index equity funds: 10–12% has been typical over long periods, with big swings year to year.
  • Hybrid funds: 8–10%.
  • Debt funds: 6–7.5%.

Use a conservative number for planning. Past returns don't guarantee future returns.

Frequently asked questions

Is the value after tax?

No. Equity fund gains above ₹1.25 lakh a year are taxed at 12.5% when held over a year (long-term capital gains). The calculator shows the value before tax.

What does "in today's money" mean?

It divides the final value by inflation, so you can see what it would buy today. At 6% inflation, ₹1 crore in 15 years buys what about ₹42 lakh buys now.

SIP or lump sum, which is better?

A lump sum invested early has more time to grow when markets rise. A SIP spreads your buying over time, so you worry less about timing. Most salaried investors use SIPs because they invest from monthly income.
