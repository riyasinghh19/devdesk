How EMI is calculated

Banks use the reducing-balance formula:

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1)

P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of monthly instalments. For ₹30 lakh at 8.5% for 20 years, r = 0.007083 and n = 240, which gives an EMI of about ₹26,035.

Why early EMIs are mostly interest

Interest is charged on the outstanding balance. At the start the balance is highest, so most of each EMI goes to interest. As you repay, the interest part shrinks and the principal part grows. That's why prepaying in the first few years saves the most.

Ways to pay less interest

  • Prepay early. Even one extra EMI a year can cut years off a 20-year home loan.
  • Choose the shortest tenure you can afford. A higher EMI over fewer years costs far less in total.
  • Ask for a lower rate when your credit score improves, or switch lenders through a balance transfer.
  • Home loan tax benefit: under the old tax regime, up to ₹2 lakh of interest a year on a self-occupied home is deductible (section 24b). See the tax calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Is this the same EMI my bank will charge?

For a standard reducing-balance loan, yes, within a few rupees of rounding. Processing fees, insurance and the pre-EMI period are not included.

What is a flat-rate loan?

Some personal and vehicle loans charge interest on the original amount for the whole tenure. A 10% flat rate is roughly an 18% reducing rate over three years, so always ask for the reducing-balance rate.
