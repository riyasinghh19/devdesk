What is the Pomodoro Technique?

A time-management method created by Francesco Cirillo in the late 1980s, named after his tomato-shaped kitchen timer ("pomodoro" is Italian for tomato). You work in short, focused rounds with planned breaks, which makes big tasks easier to start and keeps your energy steady through the day.

How to use it

  1. Pick one task. Write it down.
  2. Start a 25-minute focus round. Close chat and email, and put your phone face down.
  3. If a thought pops up, note it on paper and carry on.
  4. When the chime goes, take a 5-minute break. Stand up, stretch, refill your water.
  5. After four rounds, take a 15 to 30-minute break.

Which length should I use?

  • 25 / 5: the classic. Good for studying, email and admin.
  • 50 / 10: better for coding, writing and data work, where getting into the flow takes a while.
  • 90 / 20: for deep work on one big problem.

Frequently asked questions

Will the timer keep running in another tab?

Yes. It's based on the clock, not on counting ticks, so it stays accurate even if your browser slows down background tabs. Keep this tab open.

Why didn't I hear the chime?

Browsers only allow sound after you've clicked on the page. Press Start once and the chime will work. Also check that the tab isn't muted.

Is my count saved?

Today's finished rounds are saved in this browser only, and reset each day.
